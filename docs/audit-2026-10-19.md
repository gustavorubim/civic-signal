## P0: affects forecast accuracy before Nov 3

### P0-1 Stale and hypothetical matchups are fed in as if they were the nominee race, with no recency weighting
- **What's wrong:** the party map includes primary losers and never-candidates (Haley Stevens and Mallory McMorrow in MI, Janet Mills
  and Graham Platner in ME, John Cornyn in TX, Zach Wahls in IA, Scott Brown in NH, Thom Tillis and Lara Trump in NC, Buddy Carter in GA, Angie Craig in MN,
  Seth Moulton in MA, Bridget Brink in MI-07, Jasmeet Bains in CA-22, ...). Because of that, `votehub-race-polls-v1` maps every pre-primary
  hypothetical head-to-head to DEM/REP and uses it. The analytic backend that production runs use
  (`--bayesian-backend analytic`) then pools **all** polls with equal weight back to Nov 2024. `_posterior_logit`
  (src/civic_signal/models/polling_bayes.py:620) has no recency term. Recency (`poll_half_life_days`) exists only in the NUTS
  state-space path (inference/state_space.py:181).
- **Evidence (used polls, 10-19 raw):** Maine has 45 used polls: 20 Platner, 10 Mills, 1 generic "Dem", and only 14 Troy Jackson (the
  current nominee per the latest polls **[verify]**). Michigan has 61 used: 18 Stevens and 13 McMorrow (dated Mar 2025 to Jul 2026), 30 El-Sayed. Texas has 8
  Talarico–Cornyn. The oldest used poll ended 2024-11-29 (NC, Victory Insights, Cooper vs Lara Trump).
  Simple unweighted mean D–R of the polls the parser uses, compared with nominee-only polls since Aug 15 (including full-ballot polls the parser currently drops):
  MI −0.1 vs **+3.5**, TX +1.1 vs +2.6, GA +6.8 vs +9.1, NC +8.2 vs +10.1, IA −0.3 vs +0.2, ME +2.6 vs +2.3.
  (These are raw means with no house effects, so treat them as directional only.)
- **Impact:** the model's MI "pure tossup" (49.7%) and TX 52% are pulled toward old matchups that leaned more Republican. In polled races the effective
  sample is dominated by stale data. This is the single largest accuracy issue in the Senate.
- **Fix:** (a) add a `nominee`/`status` column to `fixtures/candidates_2026_parties.csv` and keep only polls whose D and R choices are the
  current nominees (or filter out polls ending before each state's primary date). (b) Add recency weighting to the analytic backend:
  multiply obs precision by `0.5**(age/half_life)` and use 14–21 days. (c) Optionally add a hard window, e.g. 120 days. **Effort: M** (map columns S, filter S,
  recency M + tests).

### P0-2 Party map errors and the "same-party summing" bug mis-ingest one-sided polls
- **What's wrong:** builder.py:1593 sums pct for all answers that map to the same party, so a mislabel turns a
  head-to-head into a single-party poll worth about 85–90%.
  - `fixtures/candidates_2026_parties.csv:96` Everett Wess (AL Dem) is labeled REP. Both AL polls end up as REP = 87%.
  - `:140` Larry Marker (NM) is labeled DEM. Both NM polls become DEM ≈ 90%. That's the "D-only SurveyUSA" quirk (NM 94.9%→99.5%).
  - `:65` Bob Brooks (PA-07, the Democrat) is labeled REP. F&M Brooks +5 and GBAO Brooks +4 become REP ≈ 85–90%, and **PA-07 P(D) = 2.3%**.
  - `:90` Gerald Malloy (VT-01, the Republican) is labeled DEM. `:3` Matt Schultz (AK-01) is labeled REP, so AK-01 P(D) is 0.4% **[verify Schultz's party;
    he's the head-to-head opponent of Begich, so likely DEM]**.
  - `:16-17` CA-40 Calvert vs Young Kim are both REP. If the general really is R-vs-R top-two **[verify]**, P(D) should be 0, but the model
    has 55.8%, about +0.56 phantom D seats.
  - Third-party names mapped to a major party and summed: MA Joe Tache and John Deaton both REP (`:119-120`), RI Bahry and McKay both REP
    (`:148-149`), MI Tim Long DEM (`:126`) **[verify all]**.
  - The digest/compare tooling then labels AL as "fundamentals-only (curated poll rows not usable)", which is wrong: the AL polls are in, but only on the R side.
- **Totals:** 4 Senate polls (AL×2, NM×2) and 8 House polls (AK-01×3, VT-01, PA-07×2, CA-40×2) are one-sided.
- **Fix:** correct the rows. In the parser, reject (or flag) any poll where a requested party is absent or where ≥2 answers map to the
  same major party in a two-party race. Assert this in tests. **Effort: S.**

### P0-3 Parser drops a whole poll if any candidate is unmapped, including minor-party names
- **Evidence (10-19 raw):** Senate has 479 raw polls, 60 primaries, **96 dropped for unmapped names (36 distinct names)**, 35 parsed but not modeled (FL/OH and others),
  and 288 used. House has 134 raw and **74 dropped (100 distinct names)**, which is 55% of all House polls. The drop happens at builder.py:1585 (`break` on the first
  unmapped answer). It hits the **most recent full-ballot polls** hardest. Michigan lost 8 Sep–Oct polls because of Lydia Christensen, Marsh,
  Kristy and Tim Long, and Texas lost 12 because of Ted Brown. Also lost: OH (BGSU Brown+6, co/efficient, Redpath/Levy), NC (Bray/Dublin, 9 each),
  MT (Bankhead 16, Austin 9), MN (Whiting/Simonetti), AR (Shoffner/Wadlin), NE (Ricketts **and** Osborn unmapped), and House NE-02,
  NM-01, NM-03, VA-05, WI-01 (Berman/Steil), OH-09, MN-01, ME-02, NY-21, AZ-06, UT-01..04, KY-06 and others.
- **Fix:** treat unmapped names as OTHER. Keep the poll whenever both nominees are present, ignore OTHER for the D/R options (or
  carry it as an undecided/other share), and log unmapped names. Also add missing major-party names (e.g., Nixon is mapped, but
  Stratton/Tracy IL, Graham SC opponents, House nominees). **Effort: S** (parser) **+ S–M** (name backfill).

### P0-4 Senate catalog is missing the FL and OH specials, and NE (Osborn) is modeled as a generic Democrat
- **Evidence:** `fixtures/senate_state_panel.csv` 2026 has 33 Class II states and no FL/OH. `configs/scenarios.yaml:404-407` holdovers are
  D32/R33/I2, which silently counts FL and OH as safe-R holdovers. VoteHub has 26 FL and 25 OH polls. Recent OH: CNN/SSRS Brown+6,
  BGSU Brown+6, co/efficient Husted+1. Recent FL: Moody +2 to +12 over **Angie Nixon** (the nominee per the latest polls; the SOURCES.txt
  unmapped list mentions Vindman, who's in older polls only). NE has no Democratic nominee, and Osborn (I) vs Ricketts polls are dropped (both names
  unmapped). The model gives "DEM nominee NE" **3.4%**, while the 2 recent polls are Osborn+1 and Ricketts+3.
- **Impact:** a back-of-envelope run using the 10-19 Senate draws (P(D=50 seats)=13.2%) and treating OH/FL as independent with
  P(D)≈0.5–0.65 and 0.1–0.2 moves **Senate D control from 31.4% to roughly 39–43%** (rough; ignores correlation and NE).
- **Fix:** add US-SEN-FL-2026 and US-SEN-OH-2026 rows (special, with real 2024 presidential lean and an open/appointed incumbent),
  change holdovers to R31, add an IND option for NE (Osborn), and decide how to count him for control. He's said he won't
  caucus with either party **[verify]**, so report both "D+Osborn" and "D only" control. **Effort: M.**

### P0-5 House baseline is entirely synthetic, so district probabilities aren't real
- **Evidence:** `scripts/generate_house_panel.py` docstring: "synthetic-but-plausible ... NOT actual historical House election results."
  2026 rows have 435 generic districts with incumbents 223 D / 212 R (the real post-2024 House is about 215 D / 220 R) and implausible PVIs: TX-15 D+15.3 (incumbent
  "DEM"; actually R-held), WI-01 D+2.6 (Steil, R), CA-40 D+48, CA-48 D+51, NE-02 R+38.8, MI-06 R+7.4 (Dingell seat; the model has P(D)=0.205).
  Candidates are "DEM/REP nominee XX-NN" placeholders. Results like NE-02 P(D)=2.9% and NM-03 15% contradict the D+12 and D+14 polls the parser drops.
  The Senate 2026 rows are also synthetic: incumbency flags are wrong for open seats (MI, NH, MN, IL listed as D incumbents; NC, KY, AL, IA as R
  incumbents **[verify retirements]**), WV is marked as a DEM incumbent (Capito is R), and the leans are approximate.
- **Impact:** the House control number (83.1%) inherits a +8 D-seat synthetic incumbency tilt and random district leans. 2025–26
  mid-decade redistricting (TX, CA Prop 50, MO, NC, OH, UT **[verify which maps are final]**) isn't represented at all.
- **Real replacement:** (1) 2024 presidential margin by congressional district on the **2026 maps** (The Downballot's CD
  dataset, or Daily Kos Elections), (2) incumbents/open seats from `unitedstates/congress-legislators` (CC0) + Wikipedia/Ballotpedia
  retirements, (3) 2024 House results from MIT Election Data + Science Lab, (4) for Senate: 2020 Class II results + 2024 pres margin by state.
  Replace only the **2026 rows** first (keep the synthetic history for backtests and flag calibration as synthetic). **Effort: L** (House
  ~2–3 days, including a mapping/QA script) / **S–M** (Senate 2026 rows, ~half a day).

### P0-6 National environment is pinned to a July analyst override
- **Evidence:** `fixtures/environment_override.json` sets D+2.5 (Jul 9). The measured aggregator median at that time was D+5.8
  (`fixtures/measured_environment.json`, also Jul 9 and never refreshed). Panels are baked with it: House 2026 `national_swing` = 5.0 (=2.5−(−2.5)),
  Senate = 0.5 (=2.5−1−1.0).
- **Impact:** the fundamentals prior is 3+ points more Republican than the July measured environment. That matters for the ~380 unpolled House districts.
- **Fix:** re-run `scripts/measure_environment.py`, decide explicitly whether to keep the override, regenerate the panels, and record the choice in
  the digest. **Effort: S.**

### P0-7 Duplicate poll versions are double-counted
- **Evidence:** 15 Senate and 2 House cases where the same pollster, end date and matchup appear more than once (LV+RV or two LV entries, e.g., HPU/YouGov NC,
  TIPP MI, Alaska Survey Research). Full-ballot and head-to-head versions of the same poll will both count once P0-3 is fixed (co/efficient OH).
  Generic "Dem/Rep" district ballots (MI-04, TX-23, NY-17, VA-01, AZ-06) are pooled with candidate polls.
- **Fix:** dedupe by (race, pollster, start/end date). Prefer LV > RV > A and full-ballot over head-to-head, and drop generic ballots when a
  candidate version exists. **Effort: S.**

## P1: robustness, noise, methodology

### P1-1 Monte Carlo noise and data-derived seeds
- 1,000 simulations (`configs/model.yaml:3`) give MC SE ≈ 0.0147 at p=0.31 and ≈ 0.012 at p=0.83. Week-to-week deltas of ±1–2 pts are noise.
- The posterior draw seed is `sha256(bundle fingerprint incl. polls)` (polling_bayes.py:732, polling_kalman.py:544). Every race draws
  sequentially from one RNG, so **any** poll change reshuffles every race's draws. That's why races with no new polls "move" 3–5 points (NJ-06, NC-12, ...).
- **Fix:** use a fixed seed with per-race streams (`SeedSequence(seed, race_id hash)`), or common random numbers, and raise to 20k draws
  for Senate and 10k for House (check runtime: the analytic backend took ~15s Senate / ~35s House at 1k). **Effort: S–M.**

### P1-2 Tipping-point metric is a noisy "decisiveness" count, not a tipping point
- simulation.py:862-890 counts a race as pivotal in a draw when flipping it changes control. In draws where D is one seat short, **every** race D lost counts.
  So mostly-R races dominate (10-19 top-3: IA 0.145, MI 0.144, **KS 0.140** (P(D)=14.7%)). The gaps between them (~0.005) are well under the
  pivotal-rate MC SE (~0.011, as the compare report itself states).
- **Fix:** use the 538-style tipping point (sort races by D margin within each draw and find the seat that crosses the threshold), report it with
  ≥10k draws, or rename the current metric to "decisive probability". **Effort: S.**

### P1-3 Error structure: weak correlation and probably double-counted race noise **[methodology judgment]**
- Measured on the 10-19 draws: the common (national) factor SD of D share is **2.4 pts Senate / 2.0 pts House**. Mean per-race SD is **7.0 / 13.3 pts**. Mean
  pairwise correlation is **0.10 / 0.02**. House p10–p90 width averages **33 pts** of D share.
- Causes: `residual_covariance_mode: residual_covariance_only` (model.yaml:162) zeroes the configured `national_sigma` and uses a
  covariance learned from **synthetic** backtest panels. On top of that, the simulation adds heavy-tailed local noise with tier floor sigma (A 0.035 /
  B 0.075, model.yaml:146-147; simulation.py:310) to posterior draws that already include `min_nonsampling_error` 0.035 + election-day SD.
- **Impact:** leaners are pulled toward 50% (NC Cooper +10 → 84%, GA Ossoff +9 → 77%), safe seats get spurious flip odds (MI-06), and
  low correlation understates "sweep" scenarios. That likely understates the underdog's Senate control odds and overstates the House favorite.
- **Fix:** set an explicit national + regional error (e.g., national SD ~2.5–3 pts share, regional ~1.5) and drop or shrink the extra local noise
  when posterior draws are used. Sanity-check against historical Senate/House polling error. **Effort: M.**

### P1-4 Partisan sponsors aren't adjusted for
- VoteHub gives `partisan: DEM|REP` (100 of 479 Senate polls) and `internal` (always False in this feed). The parser collapses these to `"partisan"`
  (builder.py:1606), losing direction. The model only cuts effective sample size ×0.85 (polling_kalman.py:497), which is ~4% of variance once
  the 3.5-pt nonsampling floor applies, so effectively there's no adjustment.
- **Fix:** keep the sponsor party, shift sponsored polls ~2 pts of share against the sponsor (or estimate it), and/or halve their weight. **Effort: S.**

### P1-5 `as_of` is hard-coded to election eve
- All live runs use `--as-of 2026-11-02` (scenario `default_as_of`, scenarios.yaml:400/485; `package_run.py --as-of` default; `rerun_midterms.sh`).
  posterior_diagnostics shows `max_horizon_days: 1`. The numeric effect is small today (drift 0.006 logit/√day means 15 days adds only ~0.023 logit, about 0.6 pt).
  But it's semantically wrong, it's needed once recency weighting lands (P0-1), and it would matter with a learned drift.
- **Fix:** pass `--as-of $(date +%F)` in the run scripts and default `package_run` to today. **Effort: S.**

### P1-6 Undecided and third-party handling
- D and R options are fit **independently** on raw pct (undecided left in the denominator), then renormalized per draw. One-sided rows (P0-2)
  and differing poll counts per option make this inconsistent. **Fix:** convert each poll to D two-party share (or D/R/Other) and fit one
  latent per race. **Effort: M.**

### P1-7 Silent stale-data fallback on fetch failure
- `ingest/sync.py:_sync_http` falls back to the cached raw file on any HTTP error. Weekly runs avoid this only by using fresh data dirs.
  **Fix:** surface `status=cached` and the last-fetched time in the digest and fail loudly on the live path. Also confirm VoteHub `/polls` returns
  the full set with no pagination **[verify]**. **Effort: S.**

### P1-8 Config hash and artifact fingerprints churn when the methodology hasn't changed
- `model_config_hash` changed on every run (e.g., 10-17 `14599b…` and 10-19 `fc99f8…` used byte-identical VoteHub raw files), while
  `methodology_snapshot.md` is identical across all 9 runs. The cause is data-derived keys injected into `model_config` before hashing (pipeline.py:139
  `_fundamentals_prior_rows`, plus backtest admission/horizon keys). Artifact hashes also carry lineage columns tied to the source manifest
  (the Wikipedia page changes daily) **[likely]**. **Fix:** hash only the static config and record data hashes separately. **Effort: S.**

### P1-9 Misleading quality flags
- Every row is flagged `trusted_probability` and `probability_calibration_status: fitted`, but the calibration is a no-op
  (|calibrated−raw| ≤ 1e-6) fitted on synthetic backtests. `model.yaml:40` says `backend: nuts`, but production uses analytic.
  **Fix:** flag synthetic-fundamentals races and label the calibration and backend honestly. **Effort: S.**
