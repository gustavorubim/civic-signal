fixtures/senate_state_panel.csv: provenance of the cycle == 2026 rows (audit P0-4 / P0-5)
=============================================================================================

Generated by scripts/build_senate_2026_panel.py (re-run it to rebuild; it is deterministic).
Rows with cycle 2014-2024 are the SYNTHETIC backtest panel from scripts/generate_senate_panel.py
and carry synthetic=True. They are not historical results.

Per-column sources for 2026 rows (synthetic=False)
--------------------------------------------------
- Seats (33 Class II + FL and OH Class III specials), incumbents, open-seat status,
  special flag, Cook PVI (partisan_lean, points, negative = REP):
  fixtures/ballot_2026_seats.csv, parsed from the Wikipedia article
  "2026 United States Senate elections" (wikitext snapshot sha256 e9ec0978..., fetched
  2026-10-19; CC BY-SA 4.0) by scripts/parse_wikipedia_2026_ballots.py.
- Nominees (dem_name / rep_name): fixtures/ballot_2026_candidates.csv (same article).
  Principal-candidate choices where a party has several general-election candidates:
  * Alaska REP: incumbent Dan S. Sullivan (not the second "Dan J. Sullivan" on the ballot).
  * Idaho IND: Todd Achilles. Democratic nominee David Roth withdrew (removed from the ballot
    2026-08-28; Idaho Capital Sun 2026-09-02, Tally Idaho 2026-10-13). Natalie Fleming is a
    second independent.
- Independents with no Democrat on the ballot (dem_party = IND, option suffix -I):
  NE Dan Osborn, SD Brian Bengs, ID Todd Achilles. Wikipedia's Idaho article notes NE, SD
  and ID are the three 2026 states where the Democratic nominee withdrew.
  All three say they will not caucus with either party (Nebraska Examiner 2026-10-17;
  CNN 2026-09-05; Roll Call 2026-09-24). The scenario therefore reports control both ways,
  with headline = exclude. TODO(verify) after the election.
- pres_2024_dem_two_party, pres_2024_margin (DEM minus REP, points of total vote):
  MIT Election Data and Science Lab, "U.S. President 1976-2024",
  doi:10.7910/DVN/42MVDX, file 1976-2024-president.csv (CC0 1.0), downloaded 2026-10-19,
  sha256 9936410a....
- dem/rep_previous_vote_share and previous_dem/rep_share: two-party share of the top DEM and
  top REP candidate in the seat's most recent election, from MIT Election Data and Science
  Lab, "U.S. Senate 1976-2024", doi:10.7910/DVN/PEJ5QU, file 1976-2024-senate-state.tab
  (CC0 1.0), downloaded 2026-10-19, sha256 d4492e28....
  The election used is the 2020 general, except: GA 2021 runoff; NE 2024 special
  (Ricketts v Love); OK 2022 special (Mullin v Horn); FL and OH 2022 general (Class III).
  Caveats:
  * AR 2020 had no Democrat; the top challenger (Ricky Dale Harrington Jr., LIB) is the
    DEM-side proxy.
  * WY 2020: the MIT rows have a blank party. Lummis (R) and Ben-David (D) are assigned
    from Wikipedia's "2020 United States Senate election in Wyoming".
  * LA 2020 was a jungle primary; the share uses the top D and top R only.
  * For NE, SD and ID, the IND option inherits the previous Democrat's share (proxy).
- incumbency_advantage: 3.5 when an incumbent or appointee runs, else 0.0. This constant is
  the model's convention, not data.

Carried-over SYNTHETIC fields (listed in synthetic_fields)
----------------------------------------------------------
Fundraising, economic_index, national_swing, demographic_turnout_index,
historical_turnout_rate, registered_voters and the synthetic panel-poll columns have no cited
2026 source yet. They are copied from the previous synthetic 2026 row. FL and OH, which had
no previous row, get the median of the other rows. The live config never uses the panel-poll
columns for 2026; VoteHub polls are used instead.

Known gaps / TODO(verify)
-------------------------
- Montana is a three-way race (Alme R, Bankhead D, Bodnar I). It is modeled as Bankhead vs
  Alme, and Bodnar's share in VoteHub polls is treated as other. Alme leads every poll in the
  2026-10-19 snapshot by 15+ points over either rival, so the effect on control is small, but
  this is a modeling simplification.
